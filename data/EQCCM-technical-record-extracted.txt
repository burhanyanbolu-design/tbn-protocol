Elastic Quantum Coordinate & Control Model (EQCCM)
Technical Research Record — Current Experimental State
Qiskit • IBM Quantum Fez • Quimb • Cotengra • Exact Variable Elimination / Tensor Contraction
Purpose: preserve the technical state of the research so the work can be resumed without repeating completed experiments. This document records demonstrated results, formulas, limitations, corrections, and the next controlled scaling experiment.
1. Research Question
The present research question is whether a family of quantum circuits can be constructed in which exact classical contraction difficulty grows substantially with graph/contraction structure while the corresponding quantum circuit remains physically shallow enough to execute on real hardware.
Core comparison:
Classical:  T_classical(n,w) ~ poly(n) · 2^w
Quantum hardware:  D_quantum(n) determined by gate scheduling, connectivity, routing and noise
No quantum advantage has been demonstrated. The current objective is to measure whether a meaningful divergence appears under controlled scaling.
2. Elastic / Active-Frontier Representation
The original elastic-box concept has developed into an active-frontier representation. Variables are expanded only while they remain relevant to future interactions; once they no longer connect to the unprocessed graph, they can be eliminated/collapsed.
For graph G = (V,E) and elimination order π = (v₁, v₂, …, vₙ):
Fₖ = { v ∈ Pₖ : ∃ u ∈ Uₖ such that (v,u) ∈ E }
Tₖ : {0,1}^{|Fₖ|} → ℂ
|Tₖ| ≤ 2^{|Fₖ|}
Thus a frontier containing w binary variables has at most about 2^w configurations. This is the mathematical form of the ‘magnify → use → collapse’ idea.
3. Quantum Circuit Family
The principal experimental family used two layers of single-qubit RY rotations separated by a graph-defined CZ interaction layer:
|ψ⟩ = RY^(2) [ ∏_(i,j)∈E CZᵢⱼ ] RY^(1) |0⟩^⊗n
RY(θ) = [[cos(θ/2), −sin(θ/2)], [sin(θ/2), cos(θ/2)]]
CZ = diag(1, 1, 1, −1), so CZ|11⟩ = −|11⟩
This family is useful because the interaction graph E can be modified deliberately while retaining an exactly simulable reference.
4. Exact Amplitude Formula
For final bitstring y and intermediate bitstring x:
ψ(y) = Σₓ [∏ᵢ Aᵢ(xᵢ) Bᵢ(yᵢ,xᵢ)] [∏_(i,j)∈E (−1)^(xᵢxⱼ)]
Aᵢ(xᵢ) = ⟨xᵢ|RY(αᵢ)|0⟩
Bᵢ(yᵢ,xᵢ) = ⟨yᵢ|RY(βᵢ)|xᵢ⟩
A direct summation potentially traverses 2^n intermediate assignments. Variable elimination/tensor contraction instead carries only the currently required frontier factors. Exact implementations were checked against Qiskit Statevector, with discrepancies at numerical floating-point precision (approximately 10⁻¹⁶ and smaller in tested cases).
5. Shared Multi-Output Contraction
Multiple requested amplitudes can reuse common partial contractions rather than being evaluated independently. A tree/trie-like output structure was used so common prefixes share work.
Important literature correction: multi-amplitude tensor-network reuse and tree-based reuse already exist in the literature. Therefore shared amplitude calculation alone is not being claimed as novel. The remaining research angle is output-aware/resource-constrained ordering and active-frontier management.

12. Critical Wall-Clock Runtime Correction
The most important correction came from measuring actual classical execution time rather than relying on the contraction-cost estimate.
Width-5 median contraction runtime ≈ 0.0014702 s
Harder width-6 benchmark median runtime ≈ 0.0015219 s
Runtime ratio ≈ 1.0352× (about 3.5% slower)
The width-6 runtime benchmark used the earlier full-five-extra graph (cost 38,352), not candidate-2 (cost 11,632). Therefore candidate-2's wall-clock runtime has not yet been separately benchmarked. At 16 qubits these contractions are so small that Python/library overhead dominates. This removes any basis for a quantum-runtime-advantage claim at this scale.
13. Width-7 and 20-Qubit Attempts
A 16-qubit graph with custom natural frontier width 7 was found, but Quimb/Cotengra optimized it differently: actual contraction width 10, maximum tensor size 1024, cost 19,124. This established that the custom graph-frontier metric is not generally identical to optimized tensor contraction width.
Several hand-designed 20-qubit graphs (chain plus long-range edges and denser local patterns) remained at custom frontier width 3. They were abandoned rather than spending further hardware time. More qubits or more edges alone do not guarantee classical difficulty.
14. Important Task-Difference Correction
The 32-output amplitude benchmark and the IBM observable calculation are related to the same circuit but are not the same computational task. The 32-output benchmark fixes 11 output indices and leaves five open, producing 32 amplitudes. IBM measurements estimate Pauli expectation values. They must not be described as direct runtime equivalents.
15. What Has Been Demonstrated
Exact active-frontier / variable-elimination calculations reproduce Qiskit amplitudes to numerical precision on tested circuits.
Shared-output contraction reuses common intermediate calculations.
Output-aware ordering can materially improve the custom prototype over its own greedy baseline.
Mature Quimb/Cotengra remains highly competitive and slightly faster by median in the small five-graph external comparison.
Real IBM Fez hardware reproduced meaningful phase-sensitive signals on 8-, 12- and 16-qubit circuits.
Hardware-native mapping substantially reduced physical depth and improved 16-qubit measurement agreement.
A width-6 16-qubit candidate remained executable at moderate physical depth while its contraction-cost metric increased substantially.
At 16 qubits, increased contraction-cost metrics did not translate into meaningful wall-clock slowdown.
16. What Has NOT Been Demonstrated
No quantum advantage or quantum speedup.
No exponential speedup.
No arbitrary quantum-state compression result.
No generally superior classical simulator.
No novelty claim based solely on multi-amplitude/tree reuse.
No proof that the 2^w structural scaling will become a practical wall-clock bottleneck before quantum hardware depth/noise becomes prohibitive.

18. Candidate Controlled Graph Family
One practical next family is a banded interaction graph. For n vertices and band parameter w:
E(n,w) = { (i,j) : i < j ≤ min(n−1, i+w) }
This creates a predictable local interaction width that can be varied independently of n. A quick next test can begin at 20 qubits with a moderate band (for example w = 6), use deterministic RY angles, leave five central output indices open (32 amplitudes), and benchmark Quimb/Cotengra planning and contraction execution separately. If runtime remains at millisecond scale, increase n and/or structural width before considering hardware.
19. Current Scientific Position
Current description: An investigation of output-aware, resource-constrained variable elimination / tensor-network contraction for quantum circuits, motivated by an elastic active-frontier representation, together with experimental comparison against circuits executed on IBM quantum hardware.
The decisive unanswered question is whether actual classical wall-clock runtime grows rapidly under controlled increases in optimized contraction width while a corresponding circuit remains sufficiently shallow and hardware-compatible. The next controlled scaling experiment is designed to answer that question.

22. THE BANDED FAMILY HAS PROVABLE, NOT HOPED-FOR, WIDTH

For E(n,w) = {(i,j) : i < j <= min(n-1, i+w)} the treewidth is EXACTLY w:
  * lower bound: vertices {i,...,i+w} are pairwise within distance w, so they
    form a clique of size w+1, hence treewidth >= w
  * upper bound: left-to-right elimination never holds more than w variables,
    hence treewidth <= w
Verified computationally for every band tested.

This resolves §13, where hand-designed 20-qubit graphs kept collapsing to
frontier width 3. Width is now a dial rather than a lottery, which is the
precondition for any controlled scaling study.

24. METHODOLOGICAL CORRECTION TO §10 — REPORT EFFECTIVE FIDELITY

§10 reads the two-CZ-layer result as degradation "up to 32%". Relative error on
a Pauli expectation is a misleading metric when the ideal value is small.
Converting to effective fidelity, F_eff = <P>_measured / <P>_ideal:

    experiment        2q gates    X0      X0X1    Z0X1    F_eff
    §9                      15   0.923      —     0.933   ~0.93
    §11 width-6             15   0.979    0.857   0.869   ~0.90
    §10 two-layer           30   0.822    0.434   0.681   ~0.75

The 0.434 outlier is NOT decoherence. X0X1 had ideal value 0.0267; at 10k shots
the shot-noise floor is +/-0.01, so that measurement was sampling-limited. Its
apparent 57% error is fully explained by F~0.75 plus under 1 sigma of shot
noise.

So doubling the entangling layer cost F 0.93 -> 0.75. Real, but not the 4-5x
collapse the relative-error figures imply. DEPTH IS MORE AFFORDABLE THAN §10
CONCLUDES.

Under depolarizing-type noise the relative error on <P> is approximately 1-F
independently of the observable's magnitude. Therefore F_eff is comparable
across experiments and per-observable relative error is not. Future runs should
report F_eff together with the shot-noise floor.

Back-fitted effective two-qubit error, from the above:
    0.48% per gate at 15 gates,  0.96% per gate at 30 gates
versus a 0.278% nominal median. Effective error GROWS with depth (crosstalk,
idle decoherence), so extrapolations to hundreds of gates should use >=1%.

26. HEAVY-HEX DEPTH vs OPTIMISED CONTRACTION WIDTH — THE FEASIBILITY WALL

Multi-layer family: |psi> = RY^(d+1) [CZ layer d] ... RY^(2) [CZ L1] RY^(1)|0>
Each layer is a matching from the edge colouring, cycled to reach depth d.
Widths are COTENGRA's, not our elimination order (the §13 lesson).

Design note: an initial run capped depth at the chromatic number (4 layers) and
returned a flat width of 3.0 at every depth. That was correct — a single
matching is disjoint pairs, and with d <= 4 the space-time network stays
near-1D, where optimal width is ~min(n,d). Cycling the colouring is required
for width to grow at all.

Full device, n=156, greedy optimiser:

         1         60     3.0   0 ms                 0.740    0.547
         3        167     3.0   0 ms                 0.433    0.187
         6        294     6.0   0 ms                 0.229    0.052
        10        470    12.0   14 ms                0.095    0.009
        13        588    20.0   3.6 s                0.052    0.003
        14        646    22.0   14.4 s               0.039    0.002
        17        764    28.0   15.4 min             0.022    0.000
        18        822    31.0   2.1 hr  (275 GB)     0.016    0.000
        20        880    33.0   8.2 hr  (1.1 TB)     0.012    0.000

THE WALL, QUANTIFIED:
  * At the deepest circuit where the device still returns signal (F >= 0.05,
    d=6, 294 gates), optimised contraction width is 6 — under 1 ms classically.
  * To reach even 1 second of classical cost requires ~2x the gate count, by
    which point F ~ 0.003.
  * At the 2.1-hour point, F ~ 3e-4; SNR parity with the record's 10k-shot runs
    would need ~1e11 shots.

Conclusion: on heavy-hex, the depth needed to make classical contraction
expensive costs roughly three orders of magnitude more fidelity than the
device has to spend. The TOPOLOGY cooperates — width does reach 33, i.e.
genuinely expensive territory. GATE QUALITY does not.

ROBUSTNESS: greedy returns an UPPER bound on optimal width; a stronger
optimiser finds equal or lower. So true classical cost is at most the figures
above, and the gap is at least this severe. The conclusion does not depend on
the optimiser choice.

THE FORWARD NUMBER: to reach width 31 while holding F >= 0.5 requires per-gate
error <= 0.084%. Fez's effective rate is 0.5-1%, so this needs 6-12x better
two-qubit gates — two to three hardware generations. The EQCCM framing is not
refuted; it is early.

28. IS THE HEAVY-HEX WALL ARCHITECTURAL? — TOPOLOGY COMPARISON

§26 concluded "needs 6-12x better gates", but that assumed the coupling graph
was fixed. Heavy-hex is planar and degree-3, close to worst-case for treewidth
growth, so the assumption was worth testing.

FIGURE OF MERIT. Fidelity is set by gate count G; classical hardness by
contraction width W. So the deciding quantity is width bought per gate spent:

    eta = W / G

and the per-gate error needed to hold fidelity F at target width W is

    eps <= 1 - F^(eta/W)

Larger eta means the same target is reachable with WORSE gates.

Measured at n~100, depths 1-6, cotengra greedy (script:
demo/eqccm/topology_comparison.py):

    topology            n   edges  deg  planar    eta     at width  gates
    all_to_all_sparse 100     400    8   False   0.2156        58     269
    random_4_regular  100     200    4   False   0.1750        35     200
    random_3_regular  100     150    3   False   0.1081        16     148
    hex_lattice        70      94    3    True   0.0909         3      33
    small_world       100     200    6   False   0.0804        16     199
    heavy_hex_fez     100     111    3    True   0.0732         3      41
    square_grid       100     180    4    True   0.0667        12     180

FINDINGS
  * Non-planar does beat planar, but at EQUAL DEGREE the effect is modest:
    random_3_regular / heavy_hex = 1.5x, not the 4x predicted beforehand.
    Planarity alone is therefore NOT the dominant lever.
  * DEGREE dominates. deg8 (0.216) > random deg4 (0.175) > random deg3 (0.108)
    > planar deg3 (0.073). Connectivity richness buys width far more
    efficiently than de-planarising at fixed degree.
  * Caveat: eta was measured over a shallow 6-layer sweep. heavy_hex's eta at
    18 layers is 0.038 — worse than the 0.073 shown. Treat the ordering as
    directional, not precise.


29. RETRACTION — THERE IS NO MEMORY WALL

§23 stated memory was the binding constraint (band 27 on 32 GB; 37 exabytes at
width 58). THAT IS WRONG. It assumed dense unsliced contraction.

Slicing fixes a subset of indices and contracts each assignment separately,
cutting peak memory by 2^k for k sliced indices. Measured overhead
(demo/eqccm/sliced_cost_model.py, all cases sliced to a 1.1 GB peak):

    slicing FLOP overhead: 1.00x to 3.18x

Memory costs almost nothing to eliminate. Worse for the hardness case: SLICES
ARE INDEPENDENT, so the computation is embarrassingly parallel and a cluster
attacks it with near-linear speedup. This is exactly the mechanism by which the
classical rebuttals to Google's 53-qubit claim moved the estimate from 10,000
years to days.

CONSEQUENCE: hardness must be stated in TOTAL FLOPs against a stated
adversary's throughput. Width and single-machine memory are not hardness
measures. §23's extrapolation table (band 30 -> 137 GB, band 35 -> 4.4 TB) is
retracted as a hardness argument; it remains valid only as a description of
naive dense contraction on one machine.

31. THE WHOLE PROGRAMME REDUCES TO ONE MEASURABLE QUANTITY

Everything above collapses to a single question:

    does effective two-qubit error stay near 0.5% at 300-400 gate depth,
    on a device with native (non-planar, degree >= 4) connectivity?

  * At 0.5%: degree-8 / random-4-regular circuits at depth 10 are BOTH
    classically intractable AND hardware-executable. That is the advantage
    regime, reachable with today's gate quality.
  * At 1.0%: fidelity falls to ~0.02 and there is no experiment.

EVIDENCE CURRENTLY AGAINST: §24's back-fit shows effective error GROWING with
depth — 0.48% per gate at 15 gates, 0.96% at 30. Extrapolating that trend to
400 gates gives worse than 1%, i.e. the pessimistic column.

EVIDENCE POSSIBLY FOR: that growth was measured on heavy-hex, where logical
circuits need SWAP routing. On natively-connected hardware the same logical
circuit has fewer physical gates and less idle time, so the growth may be
milder. This is untested and should not be assumed.

ALSO NOTE: the gap between Fez's NOMINAL best-quartile error (0.204%) and its
EFFECTIVE error (0.5-1.0%) is a factor of 2.5-5x, attributable to crosstalk,
idle decoherence and readout. Closing that gap is worth as much as improving
the gates themselves, and is a cheaper intervention (dynamical decoupling,
scheduling, readout calibration) than a new fabrication generation.

34. CORRECTION TO OUR OWN ANALYSIS

The first pass fitted P = (1-eps)^G and reported eps falling from 2.523% to
0.977%, flagging it as "unphysical — suspect coherent cancellation". THAT
DIAGNOSIS WAS WRONG. The model was wrong, not the data.

A mirror circuit carries a penalty INDEPENDENT of gate count: readout error on
every qubit plus single-qubit gate error. Dividing a constant penalty by a
growing gate count manufactures exactly that apparent decline.

Correct two-parameter model:
    P(0...0) = A * (1 - eps)^G      ->      ln P = ln A + G ln(1-eps)

FITTED:
    A   = 0.7409        (SPAM floor)
    eps = 0.845%        (effective error per two-qubit gate)

VALIDATION, not merely fitting: A = 0.7409 over 20 qubits implies per-qubit
readout error of 1.49%. IBM Heron's published readout error is 1-2%. The
constant term recovered from the fit independently matches the device
specification, which corroborates the model.

RESIDUALS: max 2.5 sigma across an 11.6x span in gate count (18 -> 208).
A single constant eps plus a SPAM floor explains every point within shot noise.

LESSON FOR THE RECORD: always separate SPAM from gate-scaling error. A
one-parameter fit to mirror data will always appear to show error improving
with depth.

    eps IS FLAT AT 0.845% PER TWO-QUBIT GATE. ERROR IS GATE-LOCAL.

the structural question: there is no depth-dependent blow-up, so extrapolating

37. THE REMAINING LEVER IS THE NOMINAL-TO-EFFECTIVE GAP

Measured effective error 0.845% is 4.1x the device's own nominal best-quartile

idle decoherence, and correlated readout. It is therefore addressable without
new hardware, using known tools: dynamical decoupling on idle qubits, better
gate scheduling, readout calibration, and error mitigation.

What closing it would buy, for degree-8 d=10 (395 gates):
    eps = 0.845% (today)  ->  F = 0.035    (~8M shots)
    eps = 0.500%          ->  F = 0.137    (~530k shots)
    eps = 0.300%          ->  F = 0.306    (~107k shots)
    eps = 0.204% (nominal)->  F = 0.446    (~50k shots)

So a 4x reduction in effective error turns a marginal 8M-shot experiment into a
routine 50k-shot one. That is a far cheaper path than waiting for better gates,
and it is now the highest-value engineering target.

THE CONSTRAINT WE HAD NOT SEPARATED: QUBIT COUNT.

Contraction width for an n-qubit circuit saturates at approximately n, because
time-slice-by-time-slice contraction always works, holding n indices. So the
MAXIMUM classical cost obtainable on an n-qubit device is bounded by roughly

    cost ~ G * 2^n     (G = total two-qubit gate count)

irrespective of connectivity, circuit cleverness or fidelity. Connectivity and
error only determine whether you can REACH that bound; they cannot raise it.

Against the adversary used throughout (1000 GPUs, 1e16 FLOP/s, 30 days =
2.59e22 FLOPs):

    qubits   gates    classical cost      verdict
        36    1750     seconds            trivial
        56    2000     ~4 hours           NOT hard
        64    1750     ~37 days           borderline
        70    1750     ~6.5 years         genuinely hard

NOTE ON VENDOR CLAIMS: Quantinuum's blog states H2 "departs the era of
classical simulation". By the accounting above, 56 qubits at 2000 gates is
about four hours on a serious cluster. This is consistent with the Sycamore
history, where 53 qubits was contested and subsequently simulated in days.
Treat "beyond classical simulation" claims as referring to specific algorithms
and specific classical methods, not to a general bound.

43. ERROR SUPPRESSION DOES NOT CLOSE THE GAP — §37 REFUTED

§37 claimed the 4.1x gap between measured effective error (0.845%) and nominal
best-quartile error (0.204%) was crosstalk, idle decoherence and readout, and
therefore addressable without new hardware. That claim was OUR OWN and it is
now falsified.

METHOD. Identical circuits to §33 — same 20-qubit patch, same seed, same RY
angles — rerun with the standard suppression stack:
    dynamical decoupling  XpXm on idle qubits
    Pauli gate twirling   32 randomizations
    measurement twirling  enabled
Same shot budget (5 x 4096). Like-for-like comparison against job
dadm3mjdd5gc73d7gvjg.

RESULT — every point got WORSE:
    2q gates   P baseline   P suppressed   change
          18       0.6313         0.5691    -9.9%
          34       0.5679         0.5110   -10.0%
          56       0.4719         0.4182   -11.4%
         110       0.2732         0.2695    -1.3%
         208       0.1299         0.1111   -14.5%

FITTED PARAMETERS:
                    baseline   suppressed   change
    SPAM floor A      0.7409       0.6792    -8.3%
    2q error eps      0.845%       0.860%    +1.8%

VERDICT: eps did not move (within noise). A got materially worse. The gap to
nominal went 4.1x -> 4.2x. §37 IS NOT SUPPORTED.

WHY SUPPRESSION COST MORE THAN IT SAVED
  * Pauli twirling INSERTS single-qubit gates around every two-qubit gate. At
    208 two-qubit gates that is several hundred extra physical pulses, each
    carrying its own error. The overhead exceeded the coherent-error benefit.
  * Measurement twirling applies random X gates before readout. On 20 qubits
    that is 20 extra gates immediately before measurement, which directly
    degrades A — exactly what was observed.
  * DD protects IDLE qubits, but these circuits keep nearly all qubits busy
    (uniform layer structure on a 20-qubit patch). There was little idle time
    to protect, so DD's added pulses were mostly pure cost.

METHODOLOGICAL CAVEAT, STATED AGAINST OURSELVES
Mirror circuits benefit from coherent error cancellation by construction (U
then U-dagger). Twirling deliberately destroys coherence, converting cancelling
coherent error into non-cancelling stochastic error. So a mirror benchmark is
systematically UNFAVOURABLE to twirling, and this experiment cannot cleanly
separate "twirling is unhelpful here" from "twirling removes an artifact the
benchmark was enjoying".

However: eps moved only +1.8%. If coherent cancellation were a large component
of the baseline, removing it would have raised eps substantially. It did not.
So coherent cancellation appears to be a SMALL effect, and the §35 figure of
0.845% is not materially inflated by it.

AN UNINTENDED POSITIVE: eps measured 0.845% and 0.860% in two independent jobs
under very different control settings. That stability across conditions
validates the error model of §34-§35 more strongly than a single job could.
The number is robust; only our explanation of it was wrong.

CONSEQUENCE. The remaining path on superconducting hardware of this class is
better GATES, not better control. This removes the cheap intervention §37
hoped for and leaves §40's conclusion standing unchanged: the route is hardware
with richer connectivity and intrinsically better two-qubit fidelity, i.e. the
trapped-ion class at 0.06%, once qubit counts reach ~70.

WHAT WOULD STILL BE WORTH TESTING (not done):
  * DD alone, without twirling, on a circuit with deliberately heterogeneous
    idle times — the regime where DD actually pays
  * zero-noise extrapolation or probabilistic error cancellation, which trade
    shots for accuracy rather than adding pulses
  * a non-mirror benchmark (e.g. randomised benchmarking or XEB on small
    verifiable circuits) that does not reward coherent cancellation

ADDENDUM VI — AMPLITUDE-MAGNITUDE PRUNING OF THE ACTIVE FRONTIER
================================================================
Added 2026-09-09. Classical only. No quantum time used; total for the whole
programme remains 2 jobs, 40,960 shots.

45. THE QUESTION, AND WHY IT MATTERS TO §41
§41 recorded that no verified large-width result exists, because beyond width
~50 the ideal amplitudes cannot be computed classically — and flagged that any
serious attempt needs a verification strategy designed in from the start.

This addendum tests a candidate for exactly that gap: if the exact frontier
table of 2^w entries is mostly made up of configurations that contribute
negligibly to the final amplitude, then dropping them before the next magnify
step should yield an approximate answer far more cheaply. The question is
whether THIS circuit family tolerates that, and how the tolerance behaves as
width grows.

Origin of the idea: a geometric "vector zoom / four-triangle area" intuition —
that a zoom region can be characterised by direction, distance and area, and
that regions outside a permitted boundary need not be expanded. Reduced to
something testable against real code, that becomes amplitude-magnitude
truncation: drop frontier configurations whose magnitude is small relative to
the largest currently active.

The technique itself is NOT novel. Amplitude/SVD truncation is standard
tensor-network and MPS/DMRG practice. The open question addressed here is
narrower and specific: does the banded family E(n,w) of §18 tolerate it, and
in which direction does the tolerance move with width?

IMPLEMENTATION
  demo/eqccm/banded_scaling_pruned.py — a separate module. banded_scaling.py
  is imported, not modified; every exact result already recorded in this
  document is unaffected by this addendum.

  The active frontier is held as a dict {frontier_config -> value_array over
  open y axes} rather than a dense array, so that pruning produces a real,
  measurable reduction in entries carried forward. Pruning removes dict keys
  outright. A dense array cannot show an honest saving here: zeroed floats
  still occupy the same allocation.

  Loop becomes: magnify -> use (CZ phases) -> collapse -> PRUNE.

TWO-STAGE VERIFICATION
  Stage 1 (mechanism, not idea): run with threshold = 0.0, pruning nothing.
  This isolates "is the sparse re-encoding correct" from "does pruning preserve
  accuracy". Result: max_abs_err vs the exact contract_frontier was 0.000e+00
  at n=12, bands 2/3/5/7 — exact agreement, not merely within tolerance.
  Stage 2 only proceeds if Stage 1 passes.

  Stage 2 (the actual experiment): sweep thresholds > 0, comparing against the
  exact contract_frontier as ground truth on reconstruction error, retained
  configurations vs 2^w, wall-clock time and approximate memory.

46. RESULT — ERROR IMPROVES MONOTONICALLY WITH WIDTH
Fixed threshold (level-dependent thresholding deliberately deferred; the
simpler rule was tested first so that any anomaly would have one cause, not
two). 5 open outputs throughout, so 32 amplitudes per run.

At maximum tested pruning (threshold 0.9):

  width   error      configs kept   fraction of 2^w   wall-clock
  ------  ---------  ------------   ---------------   ----------
  w=8     3.550e-03            22            8.6%      0.0016 s
  w=12    3.312e-03            22            0.5%      0.0010 s
  w=16    7.188e-04            74            0.1%      0.0081 s
  w=20    1.459e-04           104           0.01%      0.0092 s

Full w=20 sweep (n=26), for the shape of the trade-off:

  threshold  error      configs kept   fraction   time       mem ratio
  ---------  ---------  ------------   --------   --------   ---------
  0.05       5.212e-05       1432558     136.6%   128.93 s      0.683
  0.20       1.116e-04        187106      17.8%    15.95 s      0.089
  0.50       1.472e-04         14260       1.4%     0.90 s      0.007
  0.90       1.459e-04           104      0.01%     0.009 s     0.000

Two findings:

  * The error curve is a PLATEAU, not a cliff. Across every width tested,
    error rises quickly at low thresholds then flattens, staying inside a
    single order of magnitude while retained configurations fall by three or
    more orders. Reproduced independently at four widths.

  * Error IMPROVES as width grows, by 24x from w=8 to w=20 (3.55e-03 ->
    1.46e-04), while the retained fraction falls from 8.6% to 0.01%. The
    approximation gets both cheaper and more accurate as the problem gets
    harder. This is the opposite of the usual behaviour of an approximation
    and it is the direction that matters for §41's gap.

  Fractions above 100% are an artefact of measuring peak configurations
  immediately after a magnify step, before the matching collapse — the same
  transient doubling the exact algorithm's own peak accounting shows. Not a
  pruning effect.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE HARDNESS ARGUMENT — AGAINST OUR OWN CASE
This cuts against the programme's own direction and is recorded as such.
§20 and §42 quote classical cost as 2^w with a measured exponent of 1.984.
That is the cost of EXACT contraction. If a one-line magnitude cutoff reaches
1.5e-04 accuracy on 0.01% of the configurations, then the effective classical
cost of APPROXIMATE simulation of this family is far below 2^w. Any hardness
claim must therefore specify the required accuracy, not only the width —
because an adversary willing to accept 1e-04 error is not paying 2^w.

This is consistent with §23's lesson (hardness must be quoted as FLOPs against
a named adversary) and extends it: hardness must also be quoted against a named
accuracy target. It does not change §40 or §42, which concern hardware
requirements, but it does weaken the classical-hardness side of the ledger for
this circuit family.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
  * No claim of novelty. Amplitude truncation is textbook.
  * No verified result beyond w=20. Every number above is checked against the
    exact contraction, which is only possible where exact contraction is
    feasible. The monotone trend suggests continued improvement past w=20;
    that is extrapolation, not measurement. §41's verification-strategy
    requirement is NOT discharged by this addendum — it is made more urgent,
    since the approximate path now looks more capable than assumed.
  * One circuit family only. Heavy-hex, random regular and the other
    topologies of §39 are untested under pruning.
  * No threshold at any width reached 1e-06. This is a ~1e-04-accuracy
    method, not free precision.
  * Level-dependent thresholding untested (deferred by design).
  * Memory figures are approximate: Python dict overhead is not fully
    modelled, so the mem ratio column understates true overhead.
  * No physical interpretation. The originating intuition used spatial
    language (area, distance, direction), but every coordinate here is an
    abstract frontier-configuration magnitude in the algorithm's own state
    space. Nothing in this addendum measures physical position, electron
    capacity, or any real-world area, and no experimental mapping to physical
    position was attempted.

WHAT WOULD BE WORTH TESTING NEXT (not done)
  * level-dependent thresholds, per the original A_{L+1} = A_L / s^2 idea
  * the same sweep on the §39 topologies, especially real heavy-hex
  * whether the monotone trend survives an independent verification strategy
    (patch or elided circuits) at widths where exact contraction fails
  * error behaviour on observables rather than raw amplitudes, since §10
    already showed relative error on small observables is misleading

Corrections and self-limiting findings now total seven: the six of §44, plus
this addendum's finding that the programme's own 2^w cost figure describes only
the exact regime and overstates the cost of an approximate adversary.
