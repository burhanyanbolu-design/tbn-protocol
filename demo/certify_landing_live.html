AI Content Compliance: What You Need to Know

What is NY SB8420 and how does it affect advertisers?

New York Senate Bill SB8420-A, signed by Governor Kathy Hochul in December 2025, requires all advertisers to conspicuously disclose when AI-generated synthetic performers appear in advertisements distributed in New York State. A synthetic performer is any digitally created media that appears as a real person, including AI-generated models, deepfake faces, and AI-altered human likenesses. The law takes effect June 9, 2026. Civil penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each subsequent violation, enforced by the NY Attorney General. Brands, agencies, and content creators publishing ads in New York should verify their content before distribution.

What is the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirement?

The European Union AI Act Article 50 establishes transparency obligations for AI-generated content across all 27 EU member states, affecting 450 million people. Effective August 2, 2026, deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate image, audio, or video content constituting a deepfake must disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated. Providers must implement machine-readable marks enabling detection of synthetic content. The EU Commission published its Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content in December 2025, with final guidelines issued May 2026.

What is California SB942 AI Transparency Act?

California Senate Bill 942, the AI Transparency Act, became effective January 1, 2026. It requires large generative AI providers with over 1 million monthly users to offer free AI detection tools, embed provenance data in AI-generated content, and provide visible disclosure options for synthetic media. The law mandates that AI-generated images, videos, and audio carry imperceptible watermarks and metadata showing where content originated. Companies using AI-generated content in California must ensure proper provenance tracking and disclosure.

How much are the fines for non-compliance?

Under New York SB8420-A, civil penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each subsequent violation. Each undisclosed synthetic performer in an advertisement may constitute a separate violation. Enforcement is initiated by the New York Attorney General. Under the EU AI Act, member states set their own penalty frameworks for transparency obligation violations. The cost of a single compliance scan (from £29) is a fraction of even the minimum penalty.

What is a synthetic performer?

A synthetic performer is any digitally created media generated using artificial intelligence or software algorithms that creates the impression of a human performer who is not recognisable as any identifiable natural person. This includes AI-generated models in product photography, AI-created spokespersons in video advertisements, deepfake technology applied to human likenesses, AI-generated influencers used in social media marketing, and any digitally altered human appearance in commercial content. If your advertisement features a person that was created or significantly altered by AI, it likely contains a synthetic performer under the law.

Any business or individual publishing advertisements containing human likenesses in New York, California, or the European Union should verify their content for synthetic performers before distribution. This includes advertising agencies, brand marketing teams, social media managers, e-commerce companies using AI-generated product models, influencer marketing platforms, media buyers, creative studios, film and video production companies, and any organisation using AI tools like Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, or AI video generators to create commercial content featuring human-like figures.
