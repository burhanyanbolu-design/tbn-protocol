## 1. How will this research make its way into a deployed technology, product, or business?

TBN Protocol is already a deployed technology — it is live in production at tbn.hardinai.co.uk, published on PyPI, and has confirmed third-party integrations. The formal verification work proposed here adds a critical commercial differentiator: machine-checked security proofs.

The commercialisation path is direct:

**Immediate (during project):** The formally verified TBN core becomes the first trust identity system for AI agents with provable security guarantees. This positions it for adoption by regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, defence, public sector) where formal assurance is a procurement requirement.

**Near-term (12–18 months post-project):** Enterprise SaaS offering — "Verified Trust-as-a-Service" for organisations deploying AI agents in critical environments. Customers pay per-verification or via subscription tiers. The formal proofs serve as machine-checkable compliance evidence for EU AI Act and UK regulatory frameworks.

**Medium-term (2–3 years):** TBN becomes the standard trust identity layer for multi-agent AI systems — analogous to what SSL/TLS certificates became for web traffic. Revenue from enterprise subscriptions, certification fees, and commercial licensing of the verified core.

The open-source base (AGPL-3.0) drives adoption and ecosystem growth. Commercial licensing provides revenue from enterprises that require proprietary deployment or SLA guarantees.

## 2. What does the next stage of development look like after project conclusion?

If the project concludes successfully, we will have a formally verified trust verification system with machine-checked proofs of its core security properties.

**Next stage:**
- Scale the verified system to handle enterprise-grade throughput (10,000+ verifications/second)
- Build SDK libraries (Python, Go, JavaScript) with the verified core embedded
- Pursue formal certification (e.g., Common Criteria, or sector-specific certifications for financial services)
- Expand the partner ecosystem — integrate with enterprise platforms (ServiceNow, Salesforce, AWS)
- Hire a commercial team (sales, customer success) to drive enterprise adoption

**Resources needed:** £1–2M Series Seed for commercial scaling (sales team, infrastructure, certifications). The ARIA-funded formal verification work de-risks the technology for private investors.

**Who needs to be involved:** Enterprise sales lead, additional engineers for SDK development, regulatory/compliance advisor for sector-specific certifications.

**Funding:** Private venture capital (Seed/Series A). We have existing investor interest (SEIS-eligible). The ARIA grant validates the technology at the highest assurance level, making the fundraise significantly easier.

## 5. Intellectual property management

**Decision-maker:** Burhan Yanbolu (Founder & CEO, Hardin Enterprises Ltd) manages all IP decisions for the project.

**IP strategy:**
- Background IP (TBN Protocol codebase): remains owned by Hardin Enterprises Ltd, available under AGPL-3.0 with commercial licensing
- Foreground IP (formal proofs, verified implementations, specifications): owned by Hardin Enterprises Ltd
- Formal proofs will be published open-source to enable independent verification — this increases trust and adoption without compromising commercial value (the value is in the verified service, not the proofs themselves)
- Commercial licensing of the verified core for enterprises requiring proprietary deployment
- Patent consideration for novel formal verification methods applied to AI trust systems (to be assessed during project)
